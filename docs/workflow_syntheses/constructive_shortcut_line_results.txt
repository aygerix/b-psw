===== Small-exponent group structure + layered zero-sum algorithms (src/cs/smallexp)
-- mechanism : Two results. (1) Algorithmic. A 0/1 zero-sum (and target) solver for finite abelian groups whose exponent has prime factors only in {2,3,5}. It runs layer by layer over the l-adic filtrations: G/lG, lG/l^2G, and so on. For l = 2 it uses a GF(2) kernel of rho+1 elements. For odd l the new ingredient is a 'norm trick'. Take a block of rho+1 elements and a kernel vector t. Write t_i = a_i - nu*b_i with a_i, b_i squares (possible because x^2 - nu*y^2 represents every element of F_l). The block value w = sum b_i x_i then satisfies sum a_i x_i = nu*w, so the block realises every multiple of w using square coefficients. Linear algebra over rho+1 blocks then gives a relation whose coefficients are all squares. In F_3 the squares are {0,1}, which gives 0/1 relations. In F_5 they are {0,+-1}, which gives ternary relations, i.e. two disjoint equal-sum sets. Each ternary relation doubles multiplicity (m-fold to 2m-fold). Two doublings give 4-fold elements, which realise every coefficient 0..4, and a final LA step gives the 0/1 zero-sum. Per-layer cost: f_2 = rho+1, f_3 = (rho+1)^2, f_5 = (rho+1)^5. A whole group costs prod over layers of f_l(rho_{l,k}). Chaining index-2 norm steps extends this to Fermat primes l. (2) Arithmetic test of the hypothesis 'a large BPSW pool can have a small-exponent generated group'. Checked on: the real T=1500 instance; designed row sets (Pierpont q-1 = 2^a 3^b, Hamming q-1 5-smooth); and +-1-closed subfamilies (generated group of exponent 2, solvable over GF(2)).

===== Algebraic identities that make parts of the BPSW congruences automatic: universal (Chernick-type) forms, their Lucas-side mirrors, polynomial-cofactor Korselt families, several parameters, Chebyshev T_2 members and Lucas-valued parameters
-- mechanism : One-sided automation by an identity, with the other side left as a 'small residual knapsack'. Exact reduction (Theorem U). Take members p_d = 1 + d m (d in S, |S| = k, m arbitrary) and n = prod p_d. Then d p_e == d - 2e mod (dm + 2), so d^(k-1)(n+1) == N_d mod (p_d + 1), with N_d(S) = d^(k-1) - prod_{e != d}(d - 2e). Hence gcd(p_d + 1, n + 1) | N_d, and the Lucas condition at an inert p_d forces z(p_d) | N_d, a fixed integer that does not depend on m. The mirror forms: for Lucas-Carmichael members p_d = d m - 1, ord_p(2) | gcd(p - 1, n - 1) | N''_d = prod_{e != d}(2e - d) - d^(k-1). The T_2 form p_d = 2(dm)^2 - 1 is the Lucas-Carmichael form in u = m^2 with cofactors 2d^2; its Lucas side is automatic iff lcm(S)^2 | sum d^2 and m = 2 lcm(S) t, so it adds no new structure. Sign lemma: N_d != 0 for d = min S and for d = max S. So every composed-linear form (members linear in a common u = A(t), or composed with any parametrisation of m, Lucas-valued or exponential included) has at least 2 'dead' members, and for each fixed D* each dead member is a prime dividing U_{N_d}. Sharing lemma: if every member is == 1 mod m, then gcd(p_i + 1, p_j + 1) | 2(p_i - p_j)/m, so the residual moduli cannot be amortised over a pool. Automation budget: members that are all == 1 mod a and == -1 mod b satisfy ab < 2 p_max, so the automated bits are at most log2(p_max) + 1, at most half of what p^2 - 1 needs. With both sides additive and both cofactors <= D, a, b <= 2D.
